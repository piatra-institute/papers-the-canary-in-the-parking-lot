## The documentary anchor, read in full

The seed proposed *Newman v. Piggie Park* as a corrective to a nostalgic picture of the drive-in. Reading the actual record makes it something much stronger: the load-bearing evidence for the paper's central concept, because the capacity to remain was the legal category on which the case turned.

- [T1] The district court's findings describe six establishments: five drive-ins and one sandwich shop. At the drive-ins a customer "drives upon the premises in his automobile and places his order through an intercom located on the teletray"; the order is taken by an employee "who is generally out of sight of the customer"; a curb attendant delivers it to the car and collects payment; and this "is generally the only contact which any of defendant's employees has with any customer unless additional service is desired" — [@newman1966], findings of fact 2, read in full from the reported opinion — a description of a service design that has removed nearly every occasion for co-presence, written down in 1966 by a court with no interest in the point.
- [T1] The same finding records that there are "no tables and chairs, or counters, bars or stools at any of the drive-ins sufficient to accommodate any appreciable number of patrons," that "the service is geared to service in the customers' cars," that "customers are encouraged to consume the food off the premises by its service in disposable containers," and that carry-out for off-premises consumption "averages fifty percent during the year" — [@newman1966], finding 2 — this is the paper's concept of designed-away presence, documented, with a number attached.
- [T1] Finding 4 records that two of the Black plaintiffs were refused service on 12 August 1964, that discrimination at all six establishments "is uncontested and completely established by the evidence," and that "the limited Negro customers who are served must place and pick up their orders at the kitchen windows and are not permitted to consume their purchases on the premises" — [@newman1966], finding 4 — the right to buy did not entail the right to stay, in the court's own words. The paper's distinction between transactional access and presence is not an analytical invention imposed on the period; it was the operative distinction on the ground.
- [T1] The coverage question the court set itself was whether each establishment is "principally engaged in selling food for consumption on the premises" under Title II. It held that the sandwich shop was covered because customers "are furnished tables and chairs... where they may, and generally do, sit down and consume their orders within the building," and that the five drive-ins were not, since they "have no such accommodations for diners to walk into buildings to be served and to eat inside," "cater entirely to motorized customers who do not alight from their automobiles to order or eat," and serve food half of which is consumed off the premises — [@newman1966], conclusions of law — **the absence of a capacity to remain was the ground for exemption from a desegregation obligation.** This is the paper's fourth result, documented before it was modelled.
- [T1] The Court of Appeals "reversed the District Court's refusal to enjoin discrimination at the drive-in establishments" — [@newman1967], as recounted at [@newman1968] — and the Supreme Court, taking the case on the separate question of attorney's fees, stated that the district court "erroneously concluded that Title II does not cover drive-in restaurants of the sort involved in this case" — [@newman1968], per curiam, read in full from the Library of Congress facsimile of 390 U.S. 400. The reasoning was repudiated; the incentive it exposes was not repealed by being repudiated, which is the paper's point.

## The mechanism the model needs, and where its name comes from

- [T2] Appropriability, the share of the value an innovation creates that its originator can capture, and the observation that the distribution of returns depends on it rather than on how much value is created — [@teece1986], cited for the concept it names — this is the exact property that makes the capacity to remain the first thing a site cuts. A place to sit creates value largely for the people sitting and for the people they meet; almost none of it arrives at the till. The paper takes the concept and applies it to a site's functions rather than to an innovation, and says so.
- [T2] Organizational slack in the behavioural theory of the firm, and the difficulty of measuring it — [@cyert1963; @bourgeois1981] — cited to be distinguished rather than borrowed. That literature means resources in excess of what is required to produce output, held inside a firm, and treats them as a buffer or as agency cost. The slack this paper is about is not held by the firm and does not buffer it: it accrues to users. Same word, different referent, and the paper says so explicitly so that the reader does not import the wrong intuitions.
- [T2] Rationalization of consumption in fast food, and its extension into digital ordering, where the customer takes on work the operator used to do — [@ritzer1983; @ritzer2018] — the operational tradition the paper extends. Its diagnosis is not presented here as new.
- [T2] Ground rent under an existing use against ground rent under a potential use — [@marx1894; @smith1979] — the rent mechanism the model formalises. Smith developed the rent gap for gentrification and applying it to a roadside site is an extension, not a licence to call every redevelopment gentrification.
- [T2] Financialization has a narrower meaning than profit-seeking, requiring identifiable financial actors, instruments or valuation practices — [@aalbers2016] — cited as a discipline. The paper's mechanism is rent pressure and it uses that word, not the other one.
- [T2] Automobility as an interlocking system rather than an aggregation of consumer choices — [@shelleryurry2000; @urry2004; @gorz1973] — the reason a change of retail format leaves the surrounding dependence standing, which is the paper's third result stated as theory before it is computed.
- [T2] Space as socially produced rather than a neutral container — [@lefebvre1991]; the critique of perfected means with unexamined ends — [@horkheimer1947]; the dependence of accumulation on capacities its organization can undermine — [@fraser2016]; the method of reading a commercial fragment so that an order becomes legible through it — [@benjamin1999]. Cited for their central arguments; none read at page level in this pass.
- [T2] What informal public sociability requires, and what observation of small urban spaces shows about how people actually use them — [@oldenburg1989; @whyte1980] — the vocabulary for the functions the model prices. Cited for their central arguments.

## The correctives that keep this from being nostalgia

- [T2] Against locating authentic social life immediately before the present transformation — [@williams1973]. The disagreement over whether nostalgic forms confront or sedate — [@jameson1984] against [@dika2003] — used as an unresolved tension rather than as a verdict.
- [T2] The automobile as a source of relative autonomy and protection for Black travellers under segregation — [@sorin2020] — so that a flat denunciation of the car does not erase the alternatives people actually faced. Read alongside the Piggie Park record, which shows the same technology delivering the exclusion.
- [T2] Service work as work, with its own history of organization — [@cobble1992] — a fond memory of being served is not a labour history. The model prices functions and is silent about the conditions of the people providing them, which the paper states as a limit rather than papering over.
- [T2] Roadside restaurants as a sector with its own history — [@jakle1999]; drive-in cinemas as a separate case with separate economics — [@foxblack2011]. The paper keeps restaurants and cinemas apart, as the seed recommends.
- [T2] The provocation that activity need not justify itself through utility — [@bataille1988] — used to sharpen what abundance means, not to make fuel consumption a virtue.

## What the search did not settle, and what was dropped

- The seed cites Barefoot for a rise from 820 US drive-in cinemas in 1948 to 4,063 in 1958. The article could not be retrieved at either candidate location, so those figures do not appear in the manuscript and Barefoot is not in the bibliography. The cinema case is carried by [@foxblack2011] as a pointer only, with no counts.
- The seed's corporate anchors — the McDonald's 1948 self-service conversion, SONIC's current restaurant count, the Taco Bell Defy design — are first-party statements whose present content could not be independently verified in this pass, and none is load-bearing for a stipulated model. They are not cited. The paper loses nothing: it does not need a corporate press release to establish that faster collection is a design objective.
- No external emissions assessment is relied on. The burden block's parameters are stipulated and swept across three orders of magnitude precisely because no calibrated figure was established here. The IPCC urban chapter the seed cites is not used, because using it would imply a calibration the paper does not have.
- No source was found that estimates the appropriability of any of the functions the model prices. That is the load-bearing stipulation, the paper says so, and the ensemble varies it rather than defending it.
